# Your Accountability Group

A guide for members of the 90-Day Creator Sprint


## 1. Welcome

You're one of 4-6 people who are about to spend the next 90 days building something, together but apart.

An accountability group is a small circle of people who commit to the same thing: show up every week, say what's true, and help each other move. Each person brings their own goal. The group's job is to hold them to it honestly, not just cheer from the sidelines.

Success at day 90 means you moved on the thing you said mattered. It means your group helped you see clearly enough to keep going when it got hard. That's the scoreboard. Everything else is decoration.

This guide exists so your group doesn't have to figure out the basics from scratch. Read it together in your first meeting, then make it yours.


## 2. The Philosophy

Napoleon Hill wrote about the "Master Mind Alliance" almost a century ago. A small group of people working in harmony toward their own definite goals, who generate ideas together that none of them would reach alone. Two pieces of that idea are worth carrying into your group:

**Definiteness of purpose.** Vague goals produce vague conversations. "I want to grow my audience" gives your group nothing to work with. "I want to learn what kind of content actually resonates with the people I'm trying to reach" does. The sharper your stated goal, and the more it's tied to something within your control, the more useful your group can be.

**Harmony over competition.** This only works if everyone in the room genuinely wants everyone else to succeed. Sometimes that means being the person who reminds someone of their own stated intentions and gives an honest reflection of where they're missing something. A group where people perform for each other instead of telling each other the truth stops being useful almost immediately.

Everything else in this guide is just mechanics in service of those two ideas.


## 3. Forming Your Group

First meeting agenda:

- Quick introductions: who you are, what you're building
- Each person states their 90-day goal, as specifically as possible
- Agree on your weekly meeting time
- Agree on your norms (see below)
- Decide your roles (see Section 4)

Norms worth agreeing on explicitly, out loud, in the first meeting:

- What happens if someone misses one call? Do they message the group? Is anything required if they miss two meetings in a row?
- Is video on expected for the meetings?
- Is the WhatsApp group for logistics only, or also for check-ins between calls?
- If the team lead can't show up, who steps in to schedule the meeting and send the reminder?

Groups rarely fall apart from one big conflict. They fade from small, unspoken misalignments. Someone assumed one thing, someone else assumed another, and nobody said so out loud. Five minutes of explicit agreement here saves weeks of quiet friction later.


## 4. Roles

Keep this light. You're making sure two small, concrete jobs get done so the group runs smoothly. That's all.

**Meeting Lead**
Schedules the weekly call (Zoom or Google Meet), manages the WhatsApp group (you can make everyone an admin so it's not a bottleneck), and sends the reminder before each meeting.

**Timekeeper**
Runs the clock during the call so every person gets their fair turn. A good starting formula: 15 minutes per person. Roughly 7-10 minutes to share, a few minutes to receive feedback, with a little buffer built in. A 5-person group runs about 75 minutes on this formula. Your group can adjust. Some prefer a looser, more conversational pace, or booking a separate, longer call when something needs more room.

**Notes**
There's no assigned notetaker by default. Most members find it useful to keep their own log of what they committed to each week, so they can hold themselves to it. If your group wants someone to also keep shared notes, that's a welcome bonus, not an expectation.

On rotating roles: your group has a real choice here. It's good to be proactive about deciding on this.

- **Rotate every 30 days.** Everyone gets ownership experience, the load never settles on one person, but there's a little setup friction each time someone new takes over.
- **One person holds it for all 90 days.** More consistency, less overhead, but more of the load sits with one person.

Neither is correct. Decide it in your first meeting. You can always put forth a motion to rotate roles later on if you start with one person volunteering for all 90 days who changes their mind.


## 5. Meeting Structure

A simple, repeatable weekly shape:

**Open (1-5 min).** Meeting Lead kicks things off, Timekeeper confirms the plan for the day.

**Per-person turn (~15 min each).** For each member: a win from the week, a struggle, and one specific ask of the group (feedback, a question, or just to be heard).

**Group feedback window.** A few minutes after each share for the group to respond, ask questions, or offer perspective (included in the 15 minutes total per person).

**Close (5-10 min).** Each person states one commitment for the coming week. This is what next week's meeting will follow up on.

The through-line that makes this work: every share ends in a commitment, and every meeting starts by checking last week's commitments. That loop is the actual accountability.

This structure requires about 1 hour and 30 minutes for a group of 5 people.


## 6. Giving Good Feedback

- **Observation before opinion.** "You've mentioned this same blocker three weeks running" lands better than "I think you're stuck." Say what you noticed before you say what you think it means.
- **Be specific and be kind.** Kind doesn't mean soft. Honest, specific observations, delivered with care, are what people actually came for.


## 7. Asking Good Questions

The most valuable thing you can offer someone in this group is often a good question, not a good answer. A few to keep in your back pocket:

- "What would you do if you weren't afraid this would fail?"
- "What's the smallest version of this you could ship this week?"
- "What are you avoiding right now, and why?"
- "If a friend told you exactly what you just told me, what would you tell them?"
- "What do you actually need from us right now: feedback, encouragement, or just to be heard?"


## 8. When Things Get Hard

**A member goes quiet.** Reach out one-to-one before writing them off. A short "We missed you, no pressure, just checking in" message goes a long way. If they need to step back, let them do so without guilt. If someone is absent for three or more weeks, you aren't obligated to keep them in the group.

**The group loses steam.** Usually a sign the meetings have drifted from real sharing into surface-level updates. Try naming it directly ("I think we've gotten a bit complacent about holding each other accountable"), or put forth a request for more ("Could we spend some extra time brainstorming a strategic approach to what I'm working on?").

**Someone needs to leave.** Life happens. Let them go with appreciation. If your group shrinks to three or fewer people, reach out to the wider community about possibly merging with another group.

**Conflict.** Address it directly and privately first, between the people involved, before bringing it to the full group.


## 9. Quick Reference

- **Group size:** 4-6 people
- **Cadence:** weekly
- **Meeting length:** ~15 min per person (adjust as your group prefers)
- **Roles:** Meeting Lead, Timekeeper. Keep them light. Rotate every 30 days or hold for all 90, your choice.
- **Meeting flow:** Open → per-person share + feedback → close with commitments
- **Feedback:** ask first, observe before opining, be specific
- **When stuck:** ask a question before you give an answer

Section 1 · Welcome

You're not doing this alone

You're one of 4 to 6 people about to spend 90 days building something, together but apart. Each week you show up, say what's true, and help each other move.

Success at day 90 means you moved on the thing you said mattered, and your group helped you see clearly enough to keep going.

That's the scoreboard. Everything else is decoration.

Section 2 · The Philosophy

Definiteness of purpose

Vague goals produce vague conversations.

"I want to grow my audience" gives your group nothing to work with. "I want to learn what kind of content actually resonates with the people I'm trying to reach" does.

The sharper your stated goal, and the more it's tied to something in your control, the more useful your group can be.

Section 2 · The Philosophy

Harmony over competition

This only works if everyone genuinely wants everyone else to succeed.

Sometimes that means being the one who gives an honest reflection of where someone's missing something.

A group where people perform for each other instead of telling the truth stops being useful almost immediately.

Section 3 · Forming Your Group

Your first meeting

  • Quick introductions: who you are, what you're building
  • Each person states their 90-day goal, as specifically as possible
  • Agree on your weekly meeting time
  • Agree on your norms
  • Decide your roles
Section 3 · Forming Your Group

Agree on this out loud

  • What happens if someone misses one call? Two in a row?
  • Is video on expected for meetings?
  • Is the group chat for logistics only, or also check-ins?
  • If the lead can't show up, who steps in?

Groups rarely fall apart from one big conflict. They fade from small, unspoken misalignments.

Section 4 · Roles

Two light jobs

Keep it light. Just enough structure so the group runs smoothly.

Meeting Lead

Schedules the call, manages the group chat, sends the reminder.

Timekeeper

Runs the clock. Starting formula: 15 minutes per person.

Section 4 · Roles

Notes & rotation

Notes

No assigned notetaker. Most members keep their own log of commitments. Shared notes are a welcome bonus, not an expectation.

Rotating roles

Rotate every 30 days: everyone gets ownership, load never settles on one person.

One person for all 90: more consistency, less setup friction. Decide in your first meeting.

Section 5 · Meeting Structure

The weekly shape

About 1 hour 30 for a group of 5.

Open

1 to 5 min. Lead kicks off, Timekeeper confirms the plan.

Per person

~15 min each. A win, a struggle, one ask of the group.

Close

5 to 10 min. Each person states one commitment for next week.

Section 6 · Giving Good Feedback

Observation before opinion

  • Observation before opinion. "You've mentioned this same blocker three weeks running" lands better than "I think you're stuck."
  • Be specific and be kind. Kind doesn't mean soft. Honest, specific observations, delivered with care, are what people came for.
Section 7 · Asking Good Questions

Keep these in your back pocket

  • What would you do if you weren't afraid this would fail?
  • What's the smallest version of this you could ship this week?
  • What are you avoiding right now, and why?
  • What do you actually need right now: feedback, encouragement, or just to be heard?
Section 8 · When Things Get Hard

If someone drifts

A member goes quiet

Reach out one-to-one first. A short "we missed you, no pressure" goes a long way. Absent three or more weeks, you aren't obligated to keep them in the group.

The group loses steam

Usually a sign meetings drifted into surface-level updates. Name it directly, or ask for more.

Section 8 · When Things Get Hard

If someone leaves, or you clash

Someone needs to leave

Life happens. Let them go with appreciation. Three or fewer left? Ask the wider community about merging.

Conflict

Address it directly and privately first, between the people involved, before bringing it to the group.

Section 1

Welcome

You're one of 4 to 6 people who are about to spend the next 90 days building something, together but apart.

An accountability group is a small circle of people who commit to the same thing: show up every week, say what's true, and help each other move. Each person brings their own goal. The group's job is to hold them to it honestly, not just cheer from the sidelines.

Success at day 90 means you moved on the thing you said mattered.

It means your group helped you see clearly enough to keep going when it got hard. That's the scoreboard. Everything else is decoration.

This guide exists so your group doesn't have to figure out the basics from scratch.

Section 2

The Philosophy

Napoleon Hill wrote about the "Master Mind Alliance" almost a century ago: a small group of people working in harmony toward their own definite goals, who generate ideas together that none of them would reach alone.

1 Definiteness of purpose

Vague goals produce vague conversations. "I want to grow my audience" gives your group nothing to work with. "I want to learn what kind of content actually resonates with the people I'm trying to reach" does.

The sharper your stated goal, and the more it's tied to something within your control, the more useful your group can be.

2 Harmony over competition

This only works if everyone in the room genuinely wants everyone else to succeed. Sometimes that means being the person who gives an honest reflection of where someone is missing something.

A group where people perform for each other instead of telling each other the truth stops being useful almost immediately.

Section 3

Forming Your Group

First meeting agenda
  • Quick introductions: who you are, what you're building
  • Each person states their 90-day goal, as specifically as possible
  • Agree on your weekly meeting time
  • Agree on your norms
  • Decide your roles
Norms worth agreeing on out loud
  • What happens if someone misses one call? Two in a row?
  • Is video on expected for meetings?
  • Is the group chat for logistics only, or also check-ins between calls?
  • If the lead can't show up, who steps in?

Groups rarely fall apart from one big conflict. They fade from small, unspoken misalignments. Five minutes of explicit agreement here saves weeks of quiet friction later.

Section 4

Roles

Keep this light. Two small, concrete jobs get done so the group runs smoothly. That's all.

Meeting Lead

Schedules the weekly call, manages the group chat, sends the reminder before each meeting.

Timekeeper

Runs the clock so every person gets their fair turn. Starting formula: 15 minutes per person.

Notes

No assigned notetaker by default. Most members keep their own log of commitments. Shared notes are a welcome bonus, not an expectation.

Rotating roles

Rotate every 30 days: everyone gets ownership, load never settles on one person.

One person for all 90: more consistency, less setup friction. Neither is correct, decide it in your first meeting.

Section 5

Meeting Structure

A simple, repeatable weekly shape, about 1 hour 30 for a group of 5.

Open

1 to 5 min. Lead kicks off, Timekeeper confirms the plan.

Per-person turn

~15 min each. A win, a struggle, one specific ask of the group.

Group feedback

A few minutes after each share to respond, ask, or offer perspective, included in the 15.

Close

5 to 10 min. Each person states one commitment for the coming week.

Every share ends in a commitment, and every meeting starts by checking last week's. That loop is the actual accountability.
Sections 6 & 7

Feedback & Questions

Giving good feedback
  • Observation before opinion. "You've mentioned this same blocker three weeks running" lands better than "I think you're stuck."
  • Be specific and be kind. Kind doesn't mean soft. Honest, specific observations, delivered with care, are what people actually came for.
Good questions to keep in your back pocket
  • What would you do if you weren't afraid this would fail?
  • What's the smallest version of this you could ship this week?
  • What are you avoiding right now, and why?
  • If a friend told you exactly what you just told me, what would you tell them?
  • What do you actually need from us right now: feedback, encouragement, or just to be heard?
Section 8

When Things Get Hard

A member goes quiet

Reach out one-to-one before writing them off. A short "we missed you, no pressure, just checking in" goes a long way. If someone is absent three or more weeks, you aren't obligated to keep them in the group.

The group loses steam

Usually a sign meetings have drifted into surface-level updates. Name it directly, or put forth a request for more.

Someone needs to leave

Life happens. Let them go with appreciation. If your group shrinks to three or fewer, reach out to the wider community about merging.

Conflict

Address it directly and privately first, between the people involved, before bringing it to the full group.
